PROJECT 2 — Threats and Vulnerability Assessment of a Residential Infrastructure
1. Different Domains in the Residence
The residence can be divided into these domains:
1. Physical/Security Domain – doors, gates, windows, fence and locks.
2. Network/Internet Domain – Wi-Fi router, internet connection and connected devices.
3. Electrical/Power Domain – electricity supply, generator, inverter and extension cables.
4. Smart/Home Device Domain – smart TV, phones, laptops and other connected devices.
5. Environmental Domain – water, cooking gas, fire hazards and weather-related risks.
6. Human/Administrative Domain – residents, visitors, passwords, rules and security practices.
2. Possible Threats
Possible threats to the residence include:
- Burglary and theft
- Unauthorized access
- Fire outbreak
- Gas leakage
- Electrical shock
- Power surge
- Internet/Wi-Fi attack
- Malware and phishing
- Password theft
- Device theft
- Privacy invasion
- Accidental damage by residents or visitors
- Water leakage/flooding
- Extreme weather
- Social engineering

7. Mitigation Measures for Critical and Major Risks
Critical Risks
1. Burglary
- Install strong locks on doors and windows.
- Use security gates and perimeter fencing.
- Install CCTV cameras where possible.
- Keep valuable items away from visible areas.
- Restrict access to authorized people.
2. Wi-Fi/Cyberattack
- Use a strong and unique Wi-Fi password.
- Change the router's default administrator password.
- Enable WPA2/WPA3 security.
- Keep the router firmware updated.
- Create a separate guest network.
3. Electrical Fire
- Replace damaged cables immediately.
- Avoid overloading extension sockets.
- Use quality electrical equipment.
- Carry out periodic electrical inspections.
- Switch off unnecessary appliances when leaving the house.
4. Gas/Fire Hazard
- Check the gas cylinder, hose and regulator regularly.
- Keep gas cylinders away from direct heat.
- Store flammable materials safely.
- Install smoke/fire alarms.
- Keep a suitable fire extinguisher accessible.
Major Risks
1. Smart Device Attacks
- Change default passwords.
- Enable two-factor authentication where available.
- Update device software regularly.
- Remove unknown devices from accounts.
2. Malware
- Keep laptops and phones updated.
- Use reputable antivirus/security software.
- Avoid suspicious links and downloads.
- Do not install applications from untrusted sources.
3. Social Engineering
- Train residents not to disclose passwords or sensitive information.
- Verify unexpected calls and messages.
- Avoid sharing information about the home's security arrangements with strangers.
4. Unauthorized Physical Access
- Verify visitors before allowing access.
- Keep important keys secure.
- Do not leave doors or gates unlocked unnecessarily.
5. Water Damage
- Inspect pipes, taps and water tanks regularly.
- Repair leaks immediately.
- Know where the main water shut-off valve is located.
Conclusion
The assessment shows that a residence contains physical, technical, environmental and human security risks. The most serious risks should be addressed first by improving physical security, securing network devices, maintaining electrical and gas systems, and educating residents about safe security practices.
